## 1. Group 6 — Salton Sea. Not a duplicate. #379 was right, now verified.

#379 suggested moving this pair to DIFFERENT, at *strong inference from the
naming pattern*. The descriptions settle it outright `[literal]`:

| mp | source | what its description is actually about |
|---|---|---|
| `79c97694` | `atlasobscura:salton-sea` | **The lake.** *"Technically a 'saline lake,' it is saltier than the ocean… about 15 miles wide and 35 miles long."* Geology, the 1905 Colorado River breach, ecological decline. |
| `02106f71` | `california_state_parks:639` | **The park unit.** *"located along 14 miles of shoreline at the northeast corner of the Salton Sea… occupies 15,000 acres… classified as an SRA in 1963."* |

A 15,000-acre park on the northeast shore is not the lake it sits on.

**The proximity that made them look alike is an artifact.** Their centroids are
**725.4 m** apart `[literal, PostGIS]` — but only because Atlas Obscura pinned
its article at the accessible northeast shore, which falls *inside* the SRA
polygon (`ST_Within` true). Containment is not identity: the SRA polygon
measures **62.575 km²**, a small fraction of the sea.

**Decision: Group 6 stays DIFFERENT and is never merged.** It remains in the
executor's `DEFAULT_BLOCKED_GROUPS` — the block is what enforces "never merge
this".

## 2. Group 83 — Hat Rock. #379's central premise is wrong.

#379 read the two NPS rows as *"almost certainly a duplicate on the NPS side"*
and proposed a 3-way merge into the OR visitor row. That call rested on
structural signals only — both NPS, both `park_feature`, both `source_count = 1`,
one name suffixed. **It read no descriptions.** Reading them reverses it.

| mp | NPS place | what it is `[literal]` |
|---|---|---|
| `971799f0` | `hat-rock.htm` | **The rock formation.** *"a formation of exposed basalt that rises approximately 70 feet to a plateau. **It is located within the 719-acre Hat Rock State Park**"* — and it hyperlinks to `oregonstateparks.org/…parkId=12`, which is exactly `oregon_state_parks:12`, the third member. Tags: Lewis and Clark only. |
| `0f13a10f` | `hat-rock-state-park.htm` | **The park.** *"The park has its own pond, which is popular for fishing… access to a lake… a sand volleyball court near the weather shelters."* Tags add `state park`, `parks`, `fishing`, `boating`, `trails`, `Beach Access`, `hunting`. |

**The one signal pointing the other way collapses under testing.** Both records
carry the same `npmapId` (`fd52c547-…`), which reads like a same-place marker.
It is not: **299** distinct NPS place records share that id — Astoria Column,
Beacon Rock, Beaverhead Rock — and across the corpus **4,555** NPS place records
carry only **37** distinct `npmapId` values `[literal, computed]`. It identifies
the **Lewis and Clark National Historic Trail**, not a place.

**Corroboration, two independent angles:**
- NPS states the park is 719 acres (~2.91 km²); the `state_parks` polygon
  measures **2.676 km²** `[literal]` — same object, differing boundary vintage.
- `Beaverhead Rock` / `Beaverhead Rock State Park` is the identical shape and the
  corpus resolved it as **two separate master_places** (`3a3164d5`, `afc9eca6`).
  Both are soft-retired (out of six-state scope), so this is consistency
  evidence, not proof.

**The real duplicate is a different pair:** `d7ebf7be` (OR visitor) and
`0f13a10f` (NPS) both describe the park. `971799f0` is a feature *within* it.

## 3. Both groups were missing a member — the one that decides them

Neither group contained the `state_parks` GIS record for its own park, **but one
exists for each** `[literal]`:

| mp | polygon | sources |
|---|--:|---|
| `efe59e93` "Hat Rock State Park" | 2.676 km² | `state_parks:OR` + `wikipedia` |
| `5d448eb4` "Salton Sea SRA" | 62.575 km² | `state_parks:CA:park:623` |

#379 says *"None of these groups have `state_parks` on either side — that's the
whole reason they're ambiguous."* True of **membership**, but it reads as "no
such record exists", and one does for each. Under `scoreMember` (+100 for
`state_parks`) either dissolves the tie outright.

**Why the classifier never paired them** — measured, not guessed `[literal]`:

| candidate pair | trigram name sim | centroid distance |
|---|--:|--:|
| `d7ebf7be` ↔ `efe59e93` (OR) | **1.000** (identical) | 877.7 m |
| `02106f71` ↔ `5d448eb4` (CA) | **0.323** ("SRA" vs "State Recreation Area") | 13,078.4 m |

Both visitor points sit *just* outside their polygons — **5.2 m** (Hat Rock) and
**7.7 m** (Salton Sea) — which is why neither has a `contained_in` edge. That is
boundary precision, not a different place.

**Two failure modes, both worth auditing across the other 106 groups:** a name
check defeated by an abbreviation, and a distance check comparing a point to a
*polygon centroid* instead of testing containment.

## 5. How they were validated — and the limit of that validation

**⚠️ The group definitions carry PROD ids, and none of them exist on TEST**
`[literal — 0 of 7 present]`. TEST and PROD are independent corpora with
independent UUIDs. A merge of the literal ids can therefore only ever happen
against PROD.

So validation was done at two levels:

1. **Dry-run of the real definitions** — confirms the canonical pick, the
   exclusion, and the drift guard against the real source composition. Both
   canonicals resolved as predicted: `efe59e93` and `5d448eb4`, each
   *"state_parks-GIS-backed row wins (untagged GIS home)"* `[literal]`.
2. **Shape validation on TEST** (`data/scripts/verify-merge-groups-hatrock-saltonsea.ts`)
   — rebuilds each group's exact shape from synthetic fixtures (same per-member
   source composition, same polygon/point topology, and for Hat Rock the same
   pre-existing `contained_in` edge from the rock to the park), runs the real
   `merge_master_place()`, then asserts the outcome. 19 assertions, all passing
   `[literal]`, including: the excluded row byte-identical across every column,
   still owning its own `source_record`, gaining zero edges, never appearing in
   `merge_audit_log`; both merging members soft-retired; zero orphan edges; and
   positive controls so untouched-ness cannot pass vacuously.

**What this does NOT establish:** that the *specific PROD rows* merge cleanly.
Their real relationship topology is richer than any fixture, and the only way to
exercise it is the PROD run itself. Treat the shape validation as evidence about
the **definitions**, not about the corpus rows.
